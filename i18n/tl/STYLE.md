# Filipino localization style

Use clear, conversational Tagalog that Filipino students can read on the first pass. Natural Taglish is welcome when it is what students already use in class or at work. Prefer a short Tagalog explanation around a familiar English technical term over an awkward literal translation.

## Voice

- Write directly to the learner. Use active verbs and concrete examples.
- Keep sentences short enough to follow while reading a technical lesson.
- Use familiar Filipino phrasing; avoid overly formal, archaic, or word-for-word English syntax.
- Keep the tone patient and practical. Explain a new idea before relying on jargon.
- Use one term consistently across lessons, the interface, quizzes, and the glossary.

## Keep these terms in English

Keep established names and technical terms in their usual spelling, including:

`AI`, `artificial intelligence`, `prompt`, `context engineering`, `LLM`, `model`, `agent`, `token`, `embedding`, `retrieval`, `training`, `inference`, `dataset`, `framework`, `API`, `GPU`, `loss`, `gradient`, `backpropagation`, `Transformer`, `attention`, `fine-tuning`, and product, library, paper, and model names.

When a term may be unfamiliar, explain it briefly in Tagalog the first time it matters. Do not add a glossary aside to every repeated use.

## Preserve exactly

- Source code, inline code, commands, file paths, identifiers, URLs, API names, and model IDs.
- Equations, math notation, quiz correctness, answer ordering, numeric facts, and lesson metadata keys.
- Markdown structure, links and their destinations, diagram syntax, and code-language labels.

Translate the prose and learner-facing labels around these elements. Do not change the underlying lesson, answer, or data while translating it.

## Review checklist

- Can a Filipino student understand the sentence without mentally translating English syntax?
- Does the Taglish sound natural when read aloud?
- Is the technical term accurate and consistent with `glossary/terms.md`?
- Are commands, code, links, answer keys, and data unchanged?
- Does the translation explain the idea without adding a claim that is absent from the English source?
